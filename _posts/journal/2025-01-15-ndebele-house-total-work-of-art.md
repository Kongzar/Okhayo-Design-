## The Wall as Language

In the villages of Mpumalanga, there are houses that stop you mid-step.

The geometric murals of the Southern Ndebele people are one of Africa's great design traditions — a visual language developed over centuries by women, passed from mother to daughter, evolving with each generation while remaining tethered to a core grammar of triangles, rectangles, stepped forms, and bold colour fields.

## Who Paints the Walls?

It is women who paint. This is not incidental — it is foundational. The Ndebele mural tradition is a female art form, and the house wall is a female domain.

## The Geometry of Resistance

During the apartheid era, when the South African government stripped Black South Africans of land, language, and cultural expression, Ndebele women painted. The houses became louder. The geometry became bolder. The walls became a form of resistance that couldn't be legislated against.

This is design as politics. The wall as declaration.
